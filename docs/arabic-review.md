# Arabic editorial review · 5 October 2026

The Arabic text is an editorial translation of the existing English selections,
not a new translation from the hieroglyphs. This review compared every content block,
plate caption and interface label with its English counterpart. Bibliographical
citations and original Greek transcriptions remain in their original languages.

- Heissa: **هيصة** in both introduction mentions and the first plate caption.
  The previous الحِسّة was incorrect. The spelling follows the user's correction
  and is also used in [Al-Ahram's reporting from the island](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/daily/NewsPrint/326618.aspx).
- Bîgeh: **بيجة**; Philae: **فيلة**. Al-Ahram uses these names together in its
  description of the islands at Shellal.
- Ajûala remains a transliterated historical name, with the original Latin spelling
  beside it. Its identification with Abu Hor is attested in the
  [Alexandria University study of Mandulis (2019)](https://thalexu.journals.ekb.eg/article_66642_11f6b9748e11e5edaded16b436ed6232.pdf).
  That identification has not been inserted into Blackman's quoted wording.
- Improved agreement and idiom in the introduction, distinguished visitor graffiti
  from decorative inscriptions, clarified the paper squeeze used to copy the Greek
  inscription, and corrected the referent of “when I see it” in the first gate scene.
- Completed Arabic short labels for the bibliography; improved the rendering of
  associated deities in the Greek translation. Retained Sekhet and Harendotes in
  parentheses where their less familiar transliterated names first appear.
- Kept source lacunae, question marks, plate identifiers and historical claims intact.
  In particular, chronological statements still reflect the cited 1915 text;
  this review does not silently replace them with modern historical attributions.

`validate_content.mjs` verifies matching translation structure, all plate/photo paths,
and the three required occurrences of هيصة. The English and Arabic reader were
visually checked in Safari at desktop and narrow widths, including page turns.
This is an editorial and software review, not an independent specialist certification.
